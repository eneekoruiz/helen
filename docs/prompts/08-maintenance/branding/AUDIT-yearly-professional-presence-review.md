# Yearly Professional Presence Review

## Goal

Answer with evidence: if someone discovered me today for the first time, would my public presence accurately represent my current level? A cross-surface review of positioning, credibility, coherence and evolution, not a single-repository audit.

## Use when

- Once a year and after a change of role, specialization or experience; before looking for work, clients, promotion or partners; when portfolio, GitHub, LinkedIn and CV evolved separately.

## Skip when

- You only need a technical audit of one repository, or there is no permission to change the surfaces involved.

## Requirements

Mode: **REPORT** (default, changes nothing) or **APPLY** (applies safe, reversible improvements). First inventory the available sources (repository, portfolio, GitHub profile and repositories, LinkedIn, CV, domains, demos) and always separate inspected evidence, inferences and surfaces you could not access.

1. **Professional truth:** current proposition (specialty, demonstrated seniority, problems solved, audience, differentiators); verifiable facts vs unproven claims; outdated, contradictory or missing information.
2. **Portfolio:** featured projects, order, descriptions, real role, results, CTAs; links, demos, responsive, accessibility, SEO, Open Graph; screenshots and video quality; which projects to promote, update, merge, retire or archive.
3. **GitHub:** profile README, bio, pinned repositories; names, descriptions, topics, licenses, releases, READMEs, demos; abandoned or noisy repositories (keep, improve, make private, archive). Never judge by contribution volume or suggest artificial activity.
4. **LinkedIn:** headline, About, experience, achievements, skills, Featured, links; turn generic duties into demonstrable contributions only with evidence.
5. **CV:** audience, order, relevance, dates, consistency with the rest; readability and ATS compatibility first.
6. **Coherence:** name, photo, bio, tone, specialty, dates and links consistent across surfaces; first-discovery experience (trust, proof, depth, next action).
7. **Trust:** dead links, expired certificates, broken demos, abandoned domains, missing releases, weak previews, exposed personal data or secrets.
8. **Evolution:** projects that deserve a v2, a stable release or a case study; demonstration gaps (end-to-end product, complex systems, visual quality, architecture, leadership, writing, measurable impact); learning only where it strengthens the desired positioning.
9. **APPLY mode:** apply local, reversible, backed improvements first (README, docs, metadata, copy, links, assets); validate links, build, responsive and previews in a browser; group anything needing authentication or personal judgment as manual actions.

## Beyond the checklist

Think first as someone deciding in a few minutes whether to trust, interview or hire, then as a specialist digging deep. Removing noise often beats adding content.

## Limits

- Never invent achievements, titles, clients, metrics, testimonials, dates or experience.
- No publishing, archiving, deleting, making private or renaming repositories without explicit authorization; no changes to photo, identity, positioning or job-seeking signals; no contacting people or posting.
- Never pretend to have reviewed a surface you could not access; preserve URLs and SEO.

## Output

**APPLY:** Changes applied (brief, with validations), Manual actions required (surface and exact action), High-impact opportunities.

**REPORT:** executive summary; portfolio, GitHub, LinkedIn and CV reviews; brand consistency; ecosystem opportunities; Manual actions required; High-impact opportunities; priorities by impact, effort, risk and horizon; review date and next review.

In both modes, list inaccessible surfaces.
